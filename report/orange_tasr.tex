\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\small
This report studies the course Orange project derived from the EURO/ROADEF 2026
$T$-Adaptive Segment Routing challenge~\citep{orange2026}.
We implement and solve the scalar problem
$\min L=\max_{t,a}\lambda(a,t)$, that is, the minimization of maximum link utilization (MLU).
MLU is the first component of the challenge's lexicographic objective and a standard
traffic-engineering criterion used to suppress hot spots and preserve residual capacity.
All Set~A instances are solved for period~0 (1800\,s) and for periods~0 and~1 with the
reconfiguration budget (3600\,s), with averages over ten seeded runs.
Bounds, gaps and the label ``MLU-optimal'' refer only to this scalar problem,
not to full lexicographic optimality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Orange routes origin--destination demands on a directed IP/MPLS backbone.
Between routing changes, traffic follows shortest paths induced by administrative metrics,
and equal-cost paths share the flow.
Segment Routing~\citep{rfc8402} inserts a short list of waypoints; between successive
waypoints the network still uses shortest-path forwarding, so a compact header can move
traffic away from a congested link.

The EURO/ROADEF 2026 challenge~\citep{orange2026} defines $T$-Adaptive Segment Routing
($T$-ASR) over a general horizon $T=\{0,\ldots,h-1\}$.
In the course experiments we take $h=2$: period~0 is the nominal topology, and period~1
withdraws some links for maintenance while the traffic matrix changes.
A budget limits how many segments may change between the two periods.

The challenge objective~\citep{orange2026} sorts every arc-period load and minimizes that
vector lexicographically.
This report solves only the primary scalar stage of that order: the maximum link utilization.
Section~\ref{sec:obj} states the scientific rationale for that choice.
We solve all twenty Set~A instances on period~0 with a limit of 1800\,s, then on both periods
with the budget and a limit of 3600\,s.
Every table entry is a mean over ten runs with seeds 2026--2035.

\section{Data and assumptions}
\label{sec:data}

Each Set~A instance is given by three JSON files.
The network file provides vertices, directed links, administrative metrics $\omega_a$ and
capacities $c_a$.
The traffic file provides, for every demand~$d$, a source $s(d)$, a target $t(d)$ and a
volume $v_{dt}$ in each time slot.
The scenario file gives $\mathrm{maxSeg}=6$, the period-1 budget $\kappa$ and the links
removed at period~1.
Period~0 has no intervention and no budget.
Identifiers are stored as given; they are not assumed to form an interval, and no Set~A
instance contains two links with the same ordered pair of endpoints.

For a period~$t$, let $G(t)=(V,A(t))$ be the directed graph of available links.
Dijkstra's algorithm from every vertex returns the distance $d_{ij}(t)$ and the number
$\sigma_{ij}(t)$ of shortest directed $i$--$j$ paths (path counts are stored in logarithmic
form).
For an arc $a=(u,v)$, the course note~\citep{courser2026} defines the fraction of those
paths that use~$a$:
\begin{equation}
r_{ijat}=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sigma_{iu}(t)\,\sigma_{vj}(t)}{\sigma_{ij}(t)},
  & \text{if } d_{iu}(t)+\omega_a+d_{vj}(t)=d_{ij}(t),\\[0.35em]
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:r}
\end{equation}
This uniform-over-path formula is the one used throughout the experiments.
It is not identical to a router-local ECMP recursion, which can assign different probabilities
to complete shortest paths on an asymmetric DAG; loads are therefore comparable within this
report, not necessarily to an official challenge evaluator that uses the other rule.
If $j$ is unreachable from $i$, the segment $(i,j)$ is omitted.

\section{Scalar MLU formulation}
\label{sec:obj}

\subsection{Objective and scientific rationale}

Let $\lambda(a,t)$ be the load of arc~$a$ at period~$t$, that is, the ratio of carried flow
to capacity.
The implemented objective is
\begin{equation}
\min\; L
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
L=\max_{t\in T,\,a\in A(t)}\lambda(a,t).
\label{eq:mlu}
\end{equation}
Minimizing maximum utilization is a classical intra-domain traffic-engineering objective:
it pushes traffic away from bottlenecks, balances load across the topology and maximizes
residual capacity for growth, bursts and failures~\citep{fortz2002te,texcp2005}.
Queuing delay grows nonlinearly with utilization, so reducing the peak load improves the
worst-case delay bound more directly than reducing the average load alone~\citep{fortz2002te}.

The challenge objective~\citep{orange2026} extends~\eqref{eq:mlu} by continuing, after the
peak is fixed, to the second-largest load, then the third, and so on.
Equation~\eqref{eq:mlu} therefore determines the first component of that lexicographic order,
but does not optimize the remaining components.
All lower bounds, gaps and ``MLU-optimal'' labels below refer exclusively to~\eqref{eq:mlu}.

\subsection{Variables and constraints}

The binary variable $x_{dtij}$ equals one when demand~$d$ uses the segment from $i$ to $j$
in period~$t$.
On each period graph the model is
\begin{align}
\sum_{j\neq i} x_{dtij}-\sum_{j\neq i} x_{dtji}
  &=
  \begin{cases}
  1, & i=s(d),\\
  -1, & i=t(d),\\
  0, & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
  && \forall\, d,i, \label{eq:flow}\\[0.25em]
\sum_{i\neq j} x_{dtij} &\le \mathrm{maxSeg},
  && \forall\, d, \label{eq:seg}\\[0.2em]
\sum_{d\in D}\sum_{i\neq j} r_{ijat}\, v_{dt}\, x_{dtij} &\le c_a L,
  && \forall\, a\in A(t). \label{eq:load}
\end{align}
Flow conservation on the segment graph produces an $s$--$t$ chain.
Degree inequalities (at most one selected segment leaving or entering a vertex, none leaving
the target or entering the source) are added as valid cuts so that the source--target component
is a simple path.
A detached directed cycle remains formally possible; deleting it never increases~$L$, because
every segment contribution is nonnegative.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:seg} limits the header length; constraint~\eqref{eq:load} links ECMP load
to~$L$.

For the two-period model, \eqref{eq:flow}--\eqref{eq:load} are written at $t=0$ and $t=1$.
The period-1 graph deletes the intervention links, and both~\eqref{eq:r} and the volumes are
recomputed.
The symmetric difference of the two segment sets is linearized by $y_{dij}\ge 0$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_{dij}&\ge x_{d,1,ij}-x_{d,0,ij},&
y_{dij}&\ge x_{d,0,ij}-x_{d,1,ij},\\
\sum_{d,i,j} y_{dij}&\le \kappa.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
The direct routing $(s(d),t(d))$ in both periods has distance zero whenever both endpoints
remain reachable, so a feasible budget-compliant solution always exists on the tested Set~A
instances.

\section{Solution method}

The model is written in Julia with JuMP~1.32.1~\citep{jump2017} and HiGHS~1.26.0, using one
solver thread.
The recorded time is the elapsed wall-clock time spent inside \texttt{optimize!}, summed over
the solver calls of a run; graph construction and coefficient generation lie outside that timer.

If $|D|\,|T|\,|V|\,(|V|-1)\le 100\,000$, JuMP builds the compact MIP and HiGHS solves it
directly (setA-01--03 in both experiments).
Larger instances use column generation: each column is a segment path for one demand, the
restricted master minimizes~$L$, and pricing seeks a path of at most $\mathrm{maxSeg}$ hops
with negative reduced cost.
When the ordinary shortest-path weight is already nonnegative, no hop-limited path has negative
reduced cost, so the corrected master bound is valid for the linear relaxation.
Generated columns are then made binary and HiGHS applies branch-and-bound in the remaining time.

For two periods, each period is priced separately.
Because the budget only removes solutions, a certified single-period MLU bound remains a lower
bound on the joint maximum.
A candidate is accepted only after an independent check of terminals, segment limit, reachability,
repeated vertices, budget and recomputed maximum load.
A run is labelled MLU-optimal when the checked value meets the bound; otherwise it is not
certified.
The gap is $1-\mathrm{LB}/\mathrm{UB}$, and a zero gap is written only when the bound certifies
the incumbent for~\eqref{eq:mlu}.

\section{Experimental protocol}

Set~A ranges from 20 vertices, 80 links and 40 demands to 400 vertices, 2000 links and
6000 demands.
Limits are 1800\,s for period~0 and 3600\,s for the two-period model.
A run may return early when the MLU bound certifies the incumbent.
In Tables~\ref{tab:t0} and~\ref{tab:full}, MLU, \texttt{optimize!} time, gap and branch-and-bound
nodes are means over ten runs.
Status is categorical: \emph{MLU-opt.} if all ten runs are certified for~\eqref{eq:mlu}, and
\emph{Not cert.} otherwise (including mixed outcomes).
An \texttt{optimize!} mean can slightly exceed the configured limit because a call is recorded
only when it returns.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

The weakest two-period results are setA-16 and setA-19, with mean gaps $0.67$ and $0.56$
after one hour.
The best feasible value among the ten runs is better than the reported mean: $0.1025$ for
setA-16 and $0.0796$ for setA-19.
A missing MLU certificate stays ``Not cert.'', and a bound that was not computed is never
written as zero.

Two modelling choices delimit the scope of the tables.
First, the formulation optimizes~\eqref{eq:mlu}, not the full lexicographic vector of
loads~\citep{orange2026}; two routings with the same peak may still differ on the remaining
sorted loads.
Second, the split coefficients follow the course note~\citep{courser2026} rather than a
router-local ECMP recursion.
Within those choices, every row comes from the MIP above, either compact or as a path
formulation with a computed lower bound on~$L$.

\section{Conclusion}

We formulated and solved a scalar MLU model for the course Orange project on all Set~A
instances, in both the period-0 and the two-period settings.
MLU is the scientifically standard primary traffic-engineering criterion and the first term
of the challenge lexicographic objective; the report states that limitation explicitly.
Twelve period-0 instances and thirteen two-period instances are certified for~\eqref{eq:mlu}
within the prescribed time limits.
Future work could continue the lexicographic sequence on instances that certify~$L$ early,
or replace~\eqref{eq:r} by router-local ECMP if official challenge comparability is required.
