\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This report studies the primary optimization stage of the EURO/ROADEF 2026 $T$-Adaptive Segment
Routing problem. For each traffic demand and period, the model selects a bounded segment path while
accounting for shortest-path traffic splitting, scheduled link withdrawals, and a limit on
inter-period reconfiguration. The objective minimizes maximum link utilization (MLU), a standard
traffic-engineering criterion that controls the network bottleneck and preserves residual capacity.
We formulate the problem as a mixed-integer program and combine a compact formulation with a
path-based column-generation strategy to address the growth in demand--segment variables. The method
is evaluated on all twenty Set~A instances using ten seeded runs, with limits of 1800\,s for period~0
and 3600\,s for the joint two-period model. All ten runs are certified MLU-optimal for 12 of 20
period-0 instances and 13 of 20 two-period instances; the remaining cases retain explicit lower bounds
and optimality gaps. MLU is the first component of the challenge's sorted lexicographic load
objective, so these certificates establish scalar MLU optimality but do not optimize or certify the
subsequent load ranks.
\end{abstract}
